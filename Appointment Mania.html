Appointment Mania

Paste an appointment-setting transcript. Three independent agents with different starting styles (an aggressive closer, a relationship builder, and a data-and-proof seller) each pick their own approach and try to convert this lead every round, learning only from their own results. After each round the winner is decided and explained. Styles only set each agent's starting beliefs; all three face the same odds. Resets for each new transcript. Runs until you hit stop.

Detection is keyword-based, dialogue and outcomes are simulated — no AI calls.

What's real data vs. estimated

Real, sourced: the 65% baseline for "lead stays engaged / shows" and the 55% baseline for overall appointment-to-listing-signed conversion.

Estimated, not sourced: the split of that 55% between "objection handling" and "commitment ask," the risk-level adjustment per approach, and the per-objection-signal adjustments. No public dataset breaks conversion down by specific objection type or specific approach used, so these are reasoned estimates, not measured rates.

  1. Top producers convert leads to appointments at 25–30% vs. an industry average of 10–15%. Jamil Academy, "Listing Appointment Setting Scripts 2026"
  2. ~65% appointment show rate and ~55% kitchen-table (appointment-to-listing) conversion rate. Inside Sales Predictability, "How an ISA Helps You Sell 55 More Homes A Year"
  3. Cold-call-to-appointment conversion around 2%. Hacking Real Estate Marketing, "Real Estate Prospecting Conversion Rates"
These are aggregate industry averages, not calibrated to your market, lead source, or team — they're the fallback used until an approach has enough logged team outcomes (see "Log a real outcome" above). Once you have 5+, the simulation switches to your own numbers for that approach and is tagged "data" in the feed instead of "est."
